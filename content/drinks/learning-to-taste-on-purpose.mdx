Somewhere between fixing taps and running a brand, I decided to get formal about tasting. I hold a **BJCP (Beer Judge Certification Program) judge** qualification, and studied **WSET Level 2 in Wine** alongside it.

Neither of those credentials teaches you to like something more. What they teach you is language — a shared, repeatable way to describe what's in the glass, so that "this tastes off" becomes something specific enough that a brewer, or another judge, can act on it. Off-flavours have names. So do the good ones.

## Two drinks, one habit

Studying wine after years in beer wasn't as different as I expected. The categories change — tannin instead of hop bitterness, terroir instead of water chemistry — but the underlying discipline is the same: slow down, taste in a fixed order, write it down before you let yourself have an opinion about whether you like it.

It's a habit I've kept even outside a judging table. Most of what I drink now, I still taste twice — once quickly, the way anyone would, and once slowly, the way the training taught me to.
